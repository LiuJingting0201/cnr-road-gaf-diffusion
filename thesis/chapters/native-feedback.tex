\chapter{GAF representations as generative conditions}
\label{ch:native-feedback}

\section{Two representations of the same journey}
Two image representations summarize each training journey. The spatial view uses ordered Hilbert coordinates and GAF channels following trajectory-recognition methods~\cite{salvadori,ferreira,original}. The speed view applies image transforms to the journey's derived speed sequence. Separate CNNs are trained on the same five participant classes; their encoders subsequently provide conditions for trajectory diffusion.

For a scalar sequence $a_1,\ldots,a_q$, let $u_i\in[0,1]$ be its normalized value and $\phi_i=\arccos u_i$. The two angular fields used here are
\begin{equation}
 G^{\mathrm{sum}}_{ij}=\cos(\phi_i+\phi_j),\qquad
 G^{\mathrm{diff}}_{ij}=\sin(\phi_i-\phi_j).
 \label{eq:native-gaf}
\end{equation}
A third channel is a Markov transition field. If $b_i$ denotes a sequence value's bin and $P_{ab}$ is the row-normalized count of adjacent transitions from bin $a$ to bin $b$, then $H_{ij}=P_{b_i b_j}$. The angular construction follows time-series imaging~\cite{wang}. For brevity, ``GAF feedback'' refers to the implemented three-channel GASF/GADF/MTF representation, including the transition channel.

\section{Spatial Hilbert--GAF encoding}
The spatial input consists only of the ordered coordinate samples. Frozen coordinate bounds map them into the unit square, with out-of-range values clipped and counted. The recursive Hilbert function assigns a scalar index to each point. Its precision parameter is $0.0009$, corresponding to a $64\times64$ spatial grid. The temporal order is preserved: points are not sorted by Hilbert index.

The squeeze procedure compresses repeated-index runs, followed by mirror duplication when necessary, to obtain 224 values. This is not uniform time resampling or a learned encoder. If the procedure cannot reach the required length, the case is reported as an encoding failure. The scalar sequence is mapped to GASF, GADF and an eight-bin uniform MTF. The angular channels use per-sequence scaling to $[0,1]$, while the frozen channel bounds convert the three fields to an unsigned eight-bit RGB image. Coordinate and channel parameters are estimated from training data and held fixed for evaluation and generated samples.

This branch receives no additional velocity, acceleration, duration or participant feature. Nevertheless it is not invariant to motion. Different speeds change the number and order of sampled coordinates within quantized cells, and the run compression preserves some of that structure. Conversely, quantization and compression can map distinct motion profiles to the same image. On a fixed route, spatial feedback may therefore have little ability to rank the candidate pool even when it classifies trajectories from different routes well. Image hashes and the number of distinct spatial images in each pool make this limitation observable.

\section{Speed encoding and its temporal limits}
Speed is computed from the same coordinates and timestamps. Let $d(t)$ be the piecewise-linear interpolation of cumulative planar distance. At integer seconds, the speed summary is
\begin{equation}
 v_j=d(j+1)-d(j),\qquad j=0,\ldots,\lfloor T\rfloor-1.
 \label{eq:native-speed-view}
\end{equation}
These one-second increments are expressed in metres per second and resampled to 128 equally spaced relative sequence positions. The complete-cohort encoder interpolates whatever complete one-second increments are available. For a valid leg shorter than one second, it repeats the mean arc speed over all 128 positions. This declared fallback retains 2,454 training and 527 development legs in the expanded cohort.

The angular normalization is $u_i=\operatorname{clip}(v_i/35,0,1)$, with a common absolute scale across classes. Values above 35 metres per second are counted. The MTF uses eight fixed bins on this normalized interval. The two angular channels are mapped from $[-1,1]$ to $[0,1]$; the resulting image is resized from $128\times128$ to $224\times224$. Both representations then use the same ImageNet channel normalization before entering a ResNet18~\cite{resnet}.

The speed image retains absolute speed scale but compresses temporal detail. One-second averaging and the 128-point summary do not preserve all quarter-second acceleration. Explicit duration and location are not supplied to this classifier. The representation summarizes whole-trip speed structure.

\section{Recognition and the limits of score-based feedback}
The spatial and speed classifiers are separately trained on the same participant labels. Their different inputs remain separate: a speed sequence is never appended to the spatial classifier. Recognition measures participant-related information in recorded journeys. Its use for synthesis is evaluated through the conditioning experiments and independent motion metrics.

The earlier terminal experiments therefore compare matched candidates. Their scores are the floored log target-class probabilities,
\begin{equation}
 s_v(X,c)=\log\max\{f_v(c\mid E_v(X)),10^{-8}\},\qquad v\in\{S,V\}.
 \label{eq:native-feedback-scores}
\end{equation}
Single-view policies use one score and the dual-view policy uses their equal-weight mean. The fixed candidate pool and common random keys prevent a larger sampling budget from being mistaken for a feedback advantage. The later route-and-duration control distinguishes global reweighting from reweighting that preserves those marginals. For that diagnostic, weighted distributions are the mechanism endpoint: an individual coupled random selection can differ even when the marginals are preserved exactly.

A second precursor fits the motion model against frozen classifier features. This tests whether a class representation can provide a useful training target, but the classifier itself cannot adapt its representation to trajectory reconstruction. The final experiment changes that interface: GAF-derived codes become sampled conditions of the generator, and the encoder's final stage is trained with the denoiser. Terminal ranking, frozen-feature alignment and learned generative conditioning are distinct perturbations; their different populations are not pooled into a single effect estimate.

\section{Generation-trained spatial and speed conditions}
Let $E_S(X)$ be the spatial image and $E_V(X)$ the speed image. For each active view, a CNN produces a pooled 512-dimensional feature $f_{\psi,v}(E_v(X))$. A separate bottleneck projects it through widths $512\rightarrow128\rightarrow32$, using SiLU and a final LayerNorm:
\begin{equation}
 h_v=P_v\bigl(f_{\psi,v}(E_v(X))\bigr),\qquad
 h=[h_S;h_V]\in\mathbb R^{64}.
 \label{eq:g40-code}
\end{equation}
An inactive view contributes 32 zeros. The dimensionality of the denoiser interface consequently stays fixed across single- and dual-view conditions. Each view supplies a learned global condition.

In a joint variant, the final ResNet residual stage, class head and bottleneck are trainable. The earlier convolutional stages are frozen, as are all BatchNorm running statistics. Exact cached prefix activations reduce repeated image processing: their pooled teacher features and cached spatial image bytes were checked against the existing training artifacts. The input encoding remains fixed.

The 64-dimensional code passes through a $64\rightarrow128\rightarrow128$ adapter and is added to the existing diffusion-step, class, route and context embedding. Every residual denoising block receives the resulting condition at every sampling step. The adapter's final layer is initialized to zero, reproducing the common starting denoiser exactly before adaptation. The added condition therefore starts without an arbitrary perturbation of the pretrained generator.

For active views $\mathcal V$, the joint fitting objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{joint}}=
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{noise}}(\theta,\psi,P)
 +\frac{0.1}{|\mathcal V|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}
 \operatorname{CE}\!\left(c,
 g_{\psi,v}(f_{\psi,v}(E_v(X)))\right).
 \label{eq:g40-joint}
\end{equation}
The first term is the physical-time-weighted noise-prediction loss in Equation~\ref{eq:native-loss}. The class heads $g_{\psi,v}$ act on the pooled CNN features, not on the 32-dimensional bottlenecks. The auxiliary term retains participant recognition as an explicit training task while denoising can adapt features that help reconstruct motion. The fixed coefficient is not chosen from the new generation results.

Gradients update the CNN tail and projection weights. They do not differentiate the discrete Hilbert indexing, squeeze procedure or byte conversion with respect to coordinates. This distinction retains the discrete image construction without requiring a smooth mathematical inverse of its images. It also distinguishes the method from applying a classifier gradient to an intermediate noisy trajectory~\cite{cg,univ}.

Joint adaptation updates the encoder through both denoising and classification objectives. The frozen-classifier comparison measures their combined effect. Recognition scores reported for the original classifiers refer to their pre-adaptation checkpoints.

\section{Conditional Latent Diffusion Prior for Test-Time Synthesis}
Observed training trajectories supply the codes used in joint fitting. A conditional prior learns their distribution and supplies codes for out-of-sample synthesis.

After motion fitting, the final exponential-moving-average encoder is frozen and all training codes are extracted. Each active dimension is standardized by its training mean and standard deviation, with a standard-deviation floor of $10^{-3}$. A conditional latent diffusion model then fits the distribution of these codes given class, the route's 129 two-dimensional points and the existing five-component route/duration context. It uses a 256-dimensional hidden state, four residual MLP blocks, the same 1,000-index linear noise schedule, and a fixed 5,000-update budget. Its active-dimension noise loss does not train on inactive zero dimensions.

At synthesis, 50 DDIM updates sample a code from this prior; 100 separate DDIM updates generate motion conditional on that code. Route, duration and motion-noise keys are matched across the experimental model variants. The code prior has its own request-keyed random stream and cannot advance the motion-noise stream. The sampled code is held fixed through the motion denoising process. The image encoders and class heads need not run during out-of-sample synthesis.

The encoder, conditional diffusion decoder and learned code prior follow the general construction of Diffusion Autoencoders~\cite{diffae}. Here the construction operates on road-conditioned variable-length motion and prescribed spatial and speed image views. The latent prior samples the condition, and the temporal decoder generates progress.

The code prior is checked through training-side perturbations and out-of-sample code-range statistics. Pilot settings and numerical diagnostics are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:native-results}.
