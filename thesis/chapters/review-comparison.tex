\section{What a trajectory generator learns}
A route model and a motion model face different ambiguities. TrajGAIL learns sequential vehicle decisions by adversarial imitation on a road network \cite{trajgail}. Such a policy can choose among candidate links, while the rate at which a vehicle travels along a link remains a separate quantity. Diff-RNTraj jointly represents segment identity and movement within segments \cite{diffrn}. The two-stage design of RNTrajGen likewise separates road sequences from fine movement \cite{rntrajgen}. These distinctions motivate the choice of comparator here. Transferring an empirical speed sequence to the very same path gives the baseline every geometric advantage of the proposed model. An improvement in trip length under this comparison can be attributed more narrowly to motion generation.

Map-conditioned forecasting offers transferable representation ideas but uses different inference information. VectorNet encodes polylines, whereas LaneGCN explicitly propagates information over lane connectivity \cite{vectornet,lanegcn}. Knowledge of map structure can improve predictions without guaranteeing that each returned sample admits a directed lane assignment. Trajectron++ models heterogeneous agents and feasible dynamics, and Scene Transformer jointly predicts agent futures \cite{tractron,scenet}. Social GAN explicitly treats pedestrian interactions as a source of multimodality \cite{socialgan}. Motion Transformer separates intention localization from local movement refinement \cite{mtr}. All of these distinctions matter when interpreting a benchmark. A predictor exploiting an observed history, surrounding agents or inferred intentions solves a more informed task than synthesizing from a single starting coordinate. Here the route prior decides among branches and the class-conditioned motion network decides when a sampled horizon reaches each part of the route.

\section{Guiding a trajectory while it is being generated}
In sequential decision problems, Diffuser treats planning as denoising a trajectory, while Diffusion Policy generates action sequences conditioned on observations \cite{diffuser,policy}. MotionDiffuser is a closer comparison for map-based movement: it represents joint agent futures in a compressed space and guides samples using differentiable costs \cite{motiondiff}. Our inherited Hilbert transform quantizes coordinates and changes sequence length. A small change to an increment can leave every encoded pixel unchanged until a cell boundary is crossed. Consequently, scoring complete clean trajectory estimates at intermediate updates is more direct here than differentiating through the original encoder.

Classifier guidance uses a classifier gradient during the reverse process, while classifier-free guidance combines conditional and unconditional model estimates \cite{cg,cfg}. Universal Guidance extends the range of external guidance functions used at inference \cite{univ}. These methods make different assumptions about the information available at noisy states. In particular, a class score trained on clean trajectories has a physical interpretation only after a provisional motion sequence has been decoded. Inverse-problem samplers face a related distinction. DDRM uses structure in linear measurement models, whereas diffusion posterior sampling approximates a measurement term for more general observations \cite{ddrm,dps}. A participant classifier supplies neither measured future positions nor a known noise likelihood. Using its probability as a selection weight is therefore an experimental preference, rather than a statistically calibrated observation model.

Twisted Diffusion Sampling uses weighted particles to approximate conditional distributions \cite{tds}. Feynman--Kac steering makes explicit how intermediate rewards, resampling times and potentials affect reward-guided generation \cite{fk}. A terminal scorer can choose only among trajectories already produced. An intermediate scorer can change which partial solutions receive subsequent computation. That additional opportunity also permits premature loss of viable solutions. If an early estimate of the final class preference is noisy, resampling can discard good trajectories before their motions become recognizable. At a very late update, a surviving state may have too little stochastic evolution left to move appreciably away from a terminal candidate. This tradeoff motivates comparing several decision times while matching the selection weights on both sides. The finite sampler used here has one decision and does not claim the exact target distribution of a full particle algorithm.

Sampling budget itself can change conclusions. Improved DDPM studies learned reverse variances and sampling efficiency \cite{idh}; EDM separates choices of noise schedule, preconditioning and solver \cite{edm}; DPM-Solver constructs efficient numerical updates for diffusion sampling \cite{dpm}. These works motivate an explicit accounting distinction. Equalizing network calls controls one important cost, but changing which reverse step introduces randomness can change outcomes even with identical call counts. In this study each intermediate policy therefore has its own timing-matched terminal comparator.

\section{Representation, fidelity and utility}
A diffusion model over time does not uniquely specify a trajectory task. TimeGrad uses an autoregressive probabilistic model for multivariate forecasting, while CSDI conditions on observed entries for imputation \cite{timegrad,csdi}. Those problems have observed context that a synthesis request may lack. DiffWave shows that an entire ordered waveform can itself be the generated object \cite{diffwave}. For road motion the temporal target used here is more structured: nonnegative increments give progressively ordered positions on a chosen path. A plausible marginal distribution of increments can still give an implausible sequence if their temporal ordering is wrong.

Recognition offers another perspective on that loss of information. Temporal convolutional models offer an alternative to recurrent sequence encoders \cite{tcn,gru}. InceptionTime applies an ensemble of deep convolutional networks to time-series classification; ROCKET and MiniRocket show that convolutional transforms with simple classifiers can also be competitive \cite{inception,rocket,minirocket}. Unsupervised multivariate sequence representations can learn reusable embeddings without training an image classifier \cite{mtst}. These alternatives show why agreement among multiple copies of an image classifier cannot uniquely establish a class-specific motion effect. The inherited image model and raw-coordinate auditor place genuinely different restrictions on what survives encoding. In the present comparison the raw auditor remains a diagnostic model, not an oracle.

Classifier two-sample tests ask whether a learner can distinguish populations \cite{c2st}. Distributional precision and recall instead separate sample quality from coverage \cite{prd,pr}. A selector can increase concentration in a recognizable part of the data distribution while decreasing the range of plausible behaviors available to the user. For that reason we compare variation across delivered samples, time near rest and travel length in addition to speed and acceleration. Esteban and colleagues use synthetic time series to train predictors evaluated on real data \cite{tstr}. Our augmentation study uses observed examples plus synthetic examples, then tests on disjoint observed participants. This answers a practical training question whose result need not track either the classifier score or a single distribution distance.
