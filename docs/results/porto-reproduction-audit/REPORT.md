# Porto reproduction and training audit

Replayed the existing 200-epoch weights, original split, sampling seed and 3,000 published local holdout requests. The replay reproduces the archived density, OD, length and pattern metrics exactly. This is internal reproducibility. The published Porto length result (0.0242) still differs from the local 0.14404. The local sample requires 120 original observations. The paper describes that rule for Chengdu/Xi'an. The identical Porto split and length binning have not been established.

The request distance reconstructed from standardized head column 1 differs from independently summed geographic segment distances by 0.14 m at the median. Reference and generated trip lengths have medians of 13.96 km and 24.05 km. The median paired generated/reference ratio is 1.63, with Spearman correlation 0.789. On the 200-point clock the median pairwise length ratio is 2.17. Resampling reference trips to 200 points retains a median 98.1% of length. Restoring generated paths to their original point count retains 78.8%. Length JS at 10/20/40 training quantile bins is 0.1394/0.1440/0.1468. Comparing independent observed development and reserved cohorts instead gives 0.0026/0.0048/0.0055. Changing the number of bins does not remove the excess distance. The issue-15 heatmap smoothing convention changes density JS by less than one billionth.

The release notebook leaves dropout active during sampling. In 256 fixed development requests with identical initial noise per batch, eval mode gave length JS 0.2000 and notebook mode 0.2238. The dropout toggle alone did not close the gap. No mode or histogram selection was made from final scores.

The four-GPU FP32 timing comparison uses global batches of 1,024, the released guided model, AdamW, real Porto input tensors and explicit synchronization. For each setting ten timed updates follow four warmup updates. At microbatch 32 with DDP unused-parameter traversal the two bookend measurements were 2,079 and 2,179 examples/s. Disabling traversal gave 2,239; increasing the per-card microbatch to 128 or 256 gave 5,578 or 5,810 examples/s. Peak allocated memory at 256 was 8.48 GiB per card. These are throughput measurements. Changing the microbatch also changes random number grouping and dropout draws, so equivalent final model quality requires its own comparison. The original settings remain the default; --large-microbatch opts into the faster execution path.

On the 256 development requests, the median paired generated/reference length ratios at strides 1, 2, 5, 10, 20 and endpoint-only were 2.20, 1.62, 1.27, 1.16, 1.12, 1.18. The exact mean excess distances and support at every scale are in coarse/length-audit.json.

Two fresh training epochs via the opt-in fast path took 9.01 and 8.13 seconds. The original full run averaged 21.71 seconds/epoch (200 epochs, 4,342 seconds).
