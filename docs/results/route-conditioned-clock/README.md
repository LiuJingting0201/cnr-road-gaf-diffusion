# Speed diffusion with advance knowledge of the generated route

Giving the speed model the generated length and 16 distance-ordered bend descriptors produced essentially the same speed W1 as the original timing model. The changes were −0.0005 and +0.0027 m/s on 3,000 matched validation requests across the two seeds. The mean within-trip speed error increased slightly at both seeds. Destination hits were identical, as required by the fixed-route construction. At a fixed route, noise and trip request, the bicycle-to-bus switch still yielded about 24–25 percentage points more time below 1 m/s and five additional low-speed entries per ten minutes. The original clock recovered slightly more of the observed low-speed contrast, particularly at seed 42.

The common-route check is informative. On the seed-42 coordinate routes, the two independent conditioned clocks scored 0.7924 and 0.7925 m/s. Changing only the route set used for the seed-43 clock changed its score from 0.7925 to 0.6292 m/s. Arc length and trip duration largely determine achievable mean speed. For the final positions, the five-class average absolute mean-speed gap alone gives a lower bound of 0.7424 and 0.5575 m/s on the respective conditioned W1 values. Chords between sampled positions can shorten a trajectory relative to the underlying polyline. These results put the focus on the spatial model's journey lengths for future improvement.

A separate check on 5,000 training requests found median generated-to-observed polyline length ratios of 0.937 and 0.956. The corresponding median within-trip correlations between the 16 generated and observed bend totals were 0.30 and 0.29. Each observed speed target comes from a trip sharing the request with the generated route. These correlations quantify imperfect geometric alignment of that pairing. The ablation measures the joint effect of the chosen length and bend encoding on this dataset.
